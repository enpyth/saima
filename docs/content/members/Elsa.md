# Founder and President

## Elsa Yiyin Tian

Elsa Yiyin Tian is a classical vocalist, music educator, cultural project director, and academic researcher. She is the Founder and President of the South Australia International Musicians Association and serves as Vice President of the Australia China Friendship Society South Australia Branch. Her work is dedicated to strengthening the connections between music, cultural exchange, community engagement, and charitable practice. She is committed to creating professional, inclusive, and socially responsible platforms for musicians, emerging artists, and music students from diverse cultural backgrounds.

Elsa received extensive classical vocal training in Germany before completing postgraduate studies in music performance and research at the University of Adelaide. She was awarded a Master of Philosophy in 2019. She is currently a PhD candidate at the Queensland Conservatorium, Griffith University, where her research focuses on Chinese traditional opera, intangible cultural heritage transmission, children’s arts training, and institutional change. Drawing on ethnography, oral history, and performance studies, her research examines how traditional performing arts continue to evolve within changing educational, social, and cultural environments.

As a performer, Elsa has been actively involved in classical vocal performance, opera, art song, and cross-cultural music projects. She places particular emphasis on the historical, cultural, and human dimensions of musical works and continues to explore ways of connecting classical music with broader community audiences through narrative, visual media, literature, and interdisciplinary artistic presentation.

Elsa has extensive and diverse experience as a vocal educator. She previously served as a vocal tutor for the St Peter’s Cathedral Choir in Adelaide. Her students include children, teenagers, and adults, and her teaching encompasses classical singing, musical theatre, contemporary and popular vocal styles, and stage performance. She develops individualised training programs based on each student’s voice, age, artistic interests, and learning goals. Her teaching combines healthy vocal technique with musical interpretation, language, communication, stagecraft, and the development of an individual artistic identity.

Elsa regularly prepares students for South Australian Eisteddfod competitions and other vocal and performance events. Each year, her students collectively receive more than 25 awards across a range of age groups and categories, including classical voice, musical theatre, and popular singing. Many of her students also perform in school productions, festivals, community events, and association concerts, developing into confident young performers with strong vocal skills, musical understanding, and stage presence. Elsa believes that music education extends beyond technical training. It should help students understand their own voices, develop healthy and sustainable singing habits, and build confidence, creativity, aesthetic awareness, communication skills, and empathy.

Elsa is also an experienced cultural event organiser. She has conceived, directed, and delivered major concerts, community performances, cross-cultural initiatives, and charitable projects. These include the 2024 concert *Impressions of China* and the 2025 production *A Journey of Brilliance: From the Silk Road to the Renaissance*, presented at Elder Hall, the University of Adelaide. She has also led performances by musicians and students in aged-care facilities, community organisations, and charitable settings. Her projects often combine music, history, cultural storytelling, and visual presentation while bringing together artists from diverse professional and cultural backgrounds.

Under her leadership, the South Australia International Musicians Association has developed a strong commitment not only to high-quality performance, but also to community service and charitable engagement. The Association has presented a children’s charity concert and fundraising sale in support of Adelaide Zoo and has organised performances in aged-care homes, community centres, and charitable organisations. Elsa has also promoted parent-child singing initiatives, emerging artist showcases, volunteer participation, and intercultural collaboration, with the aim of making music a shared language across generations, cultures, and life experiences.

Elsa founded the South Australia International Musicians Association to create an arts platform centred on musicians, music students, and the wider community. She believes that art should not be confined to the concert stage or limited by culture, identity, or age. Music can preserve history and cultural memory, foster understanding, support healing, strengthen communities, and encourage people to engage with one another with greater empathy.

Looking ahead, Elsa will continue to lead the Association in developing high-quality musical projects, emerging artist development, intercultural exchange, charitable performance, and academic collaboration, contributing to a vibrant and inclusive multicultural arts community in South Australia.
